A regression caught before it shipped, same pattern as the rebrand/rebrands
bug in v2. Adding "ban" (left-anchored, like every other keyword) initially
matched "Bank"/"banking" too -- "ban" is a left-anchored prefix of "bank"
the same way "valuation" is a substring of "Evaluation" -- which would have
mislabeled nearly every Chime post (Chime *is* a bank) as `regulatory`.
Caught by testing `classify_category("Chime is a great banking app")`
before committing; fixed with a full \b...\b boundary for this one specific
keyword (pipeline/classify/category.py, `_FULL_BOUNDARY_KEYWORDS`), leaving
every other keyword's intentional left-anchor stem-matching untouched.

v3.1 -- two general bug fixes, found by root-causing specific v3 mismatches
but verified dataset-wide before being called fixes (not tuned to just
these rows). No new keywords/taxonomy added this round.
================================================================

1. Smart-quote normalization (pipeline/classify/sentiment.py). Root-caused
   why [24894880] "Oatly Slams EU over 'dairy ban'" scored neutral despite
   "ban" being a real, strongly negative VADER lexicon word (-0.5574 in
   isolation): HN's title uses a Unicode curly closing quote (U+2019) glued
   to "ban", and VADER's tokenizer treats "ban'" (curly) as a different,
   unrecognized token from "ban" -- confirmed directly:
   polarity_scores("ban'") [straight quote] = -0.5574, polarity_scores(
   "ban'") [curly quote, U+2019] = 0.0. Checked how many records this could
   plausibly affect before calling it "fixed": 11/240 (4.6%) contain a
   smart quote or apostrophe anywhere in title+text. Fix: translate smart
   punctuation to ASCII equivalents before scoring (a small explicit map,
   not a blanket Unicode normalization that could distort real words).

2. Full word-boundary for "fee" (pipeline/classify/category.py), same bug
   class as "ban"/"bank" in v3, found a second time -- this time by
   auditing every real "\bfee" hit in the dataset rather than by
   inspection: 13 hits total, 11 were "feedback"/"feel"/"feels"/"feeling"
   (false positives from "fee" being a left-anchored prefix), only 2 were a
   real "fee" ("license fee of $0.", "form fee (still..."). Checked "fees"
   (plural) doesn't appear anywhere in the dataset before adding it to
   `_FULL_BOUNDARY_KEYWORDS` -- confirmed nothing real was lost.
